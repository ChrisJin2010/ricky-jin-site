Teaching in Xishan

In the summer of 2024, I arrived in Xishan Township, Eryuan, in the mountains of Yunnan — and the reality of rural education hit me hard. Thirty-two students shared a dormitory smaller than 40 square meters; the entire school owned three aging projectors; 80% of the students had never once left their county. That eight-day trip with the Urban Aid Alliance became the start of something I have not put down since.

I taught English through alphabet-guessing games, captained soccer training for the U15 team, and co-directed the students' first-ever play, The Little Girl Who Sold Charcoal. Watching shy observers become confident performers — and hearing parents erupt in tearful applause at the final bow — taught me more than any lesson I had planned. In the pine forests, my students became my teachers, showing me how to identify the wild mushrooms that grew there.

One eight-day trip has since grown into a three-year, multi-school alliance spanning Shanghai and Nanjing, bringing together volunteers from international and domestic schools. The curriculum now runs from English, soccer, and drama to math, chess, and paper quilling, and our fundraising campaigns have delivered over $10,000 in funds and supplies. Mountains separate us from Xishan; they do not separate us from each other.
